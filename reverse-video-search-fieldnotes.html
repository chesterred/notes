Reverse video search in practice: three clips, three different answers

2026-11-26 · working notes

The movie scene resolved in seconds to the correct title and timestamp. That is the easy case: the frame has distinctive faces and production design, and the index clearly covers commercial film. The result page linked a clip database entry, not a streaming page, which is fine for attribution work.

The screen recording returned nothing, as expected — no index covers private footage. Upload limits are the other constraint: files over a few hundred megabytes are rejected. The supported formats and size caps are listed on Reverse Video Search, and they matter more than the accuracy claims for day-to-day use.

The TikTok repost was harder. It found near-duplicates but not the original uploader, because the clip had been cropped and re-coloured. Cropping seems to break the perceptual hash more than colour grading does. For takedown or credit requests, the near-duplicate list is still useful as a starting point.

Verdict: genuinely useful for film and viral clips, useless for private recordings. Treat it as a lead generator, not proof.
