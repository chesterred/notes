Kling 4 test: motion quality versus prompt obedience

2026-11-26 · working notes

On anything involving a body in motion — running, dancing, turning — Kling 4 kept limb count and joint direction correct far more often. That alone justifies using it for people. The cost is obedience: it will sometimes ignore a framing instruction in favour of a more dynamic composition, which is great for b-roll and bad for a locked shot.

The interface is Korean-first with an English toggle, and the credit system is transparent about what each duration costs. Rates and plan comparisons are on Kling 4, which I checked before committing to a monthly tier.

Text and logos are still unreliable, and Korean text in particular tends to come out as plausible-looking shapes. For a title card, generate the background and composite the type. Image-to-video is the better mode for product work: give it a clean still and the model adds motion without redesigning the object.

Verdict: my pick for human motion; not my pick for precise framing or on-screen text.
